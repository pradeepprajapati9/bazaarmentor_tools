One developer, not an agency

The person who writes the code is the person who picks up your call.

I am Pradeep. I write ERP software for a hospital by day — the unglamorous kind that has to work on a Monday morning with a queue at the counter — and I build products of my own the rest of the time.

That day job is the reason I understand small businesses. Hospital software is not about clever features; it is about a receptionist who has thirty seconds per patient and no patience for a system that hangs. A shop counter is the same. So I build for the person actually using it, not for a demo.

The projects on the work page are mine end to end — idea, code, deployment, and the boring maintenance afterwards. One of them went through Shopify's own app review and is listed publicly in their App Store. Another has been running unattended every trading day. Nothing there is a tutorial project.

On using AI

I use AI tools to build faster, and I would rather say that plainly than pretend otherwise. It is why a working demo takes a week instead of two months.

What it does not mean is shipping code I have not read or tested. Security, correctness and keeping your data safe are still my responsibility, and I treat them that way. You are hiring a developer who is accountable, not a prompt.

What I will not do

  • Promise something I have not built before and know I cannot finish.
  • Hold your data hostage. It is exported and handed over whenever you ask.
  • Disappear after delivery. If it breaks, it is mine to fix.
